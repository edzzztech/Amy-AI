Amy AI — local-first desktop assistantYour computer, talked to.

Amy AI is a voice assistant that runs on your own machine. She hears you across the room, looks at whatever is on your screen, drives your apps for you, and models real CAD parts — with the language models running locally through Ollama, so nothing is shipped off to an API.

Capabilities

What a local AI assistant can actually do

Amy has hands, eyes and ears on the machine she runs on. Every subsystem below is part of the same process — one wake word reaches all of them.

Full-duplex voice

She listens while she speaks. Interrupt her mid-sentence and she stops — barge-in is voice-activated, with a clap as a backup wake signal.

Read more

Amy listens and speaks at the same time. There is no press-to-talk and no waiting for her to finish — start speaking and she stops mid-sentence.

How to use it

  • Say the wake word, then your request: “Amy, what’s the weather?”
  • Interrupt her at any point by simply talking over her.
  • Clap twice to wake her if your voice won’t carry.
  • Pause mid-thought and she keeps listening rather than cutting you off.

Settings

wake_wordWhat she answers to. Pick something that won’t appear in normal conversation.
barge_invoice stops her the moment you speak; off lets her finish.
barge_sensitivityRaise it if background noise keeps cutting her off.
clap_wakeClap detection as a backup wake signal.
whisper_modelsmall.en by default. Larger is more accurate and slower.
continuation_listeningKeeps the mic open if you paused rather than finished.

Worth knowing

Install faster-whisper and she uses it automatically for offline recognition — noticeably more accurate than the default. Voice activity detection gates the expensive stages, so the transcriber only runs on real speech.

Screen awareness

A vision model reads your actual display on an interval. Ask what's on screen, what an error means, or what that dialog is asking for.

Read more

A local vision model looks at your actual display, so you can ask about what you are looking at instead of describing it.

How to use it

  • “Amy, what am I looking at?”
  • “What does this error mean?”
  • “What is this dialog asking me?”
  • Double-click the desktop orb to make her look at the screen immediately.

Settings

vision_intervalSeconds between automatic looks. Lower is more responsive and more expensive.
screen_awareness_on_startOff by default — she only looks when asked.
lens_tokensReply length for vision answers. Short answers are much faster.
warm_visionPreloads the vision model at startup so the first look is not slow.

Worth knowing

This needs a vision-capable model pulled in Ollama — llava by default. Screen captures are processed locally and never leave the machine.

Computer control

An agent that finds and drives real applications through UI automation — opening, clicking, typing. Risky steps wait for your approval first.

Read more

Amy builds an index of the applications on your machine, then finds and drives them through Windows UI automation — opening, clicking, typing, navigating.

How to use it

  • “Open Chrome and search for CadQuery docs.”
  • “When I say my editor, I mean VS Code” — teaches an alias she remembers.
  • “How many apps can you see?” — rebuilds the app index.

Settings

automation_approvalrisky gates destructive steps only; all asks before everything.
approval_timeoutSeconds to wait for your answer. Timing out declines.
allow_shell_commandsOff by default, deliberately. Only enable if you want her running shell commands.

Worth knowing

Approval prompts appear in the window and are spoken. Leaving automation_approval at risky is the sensible default — she acts freely on safe steps and stops for anything that deletes, sends or overwrites.

Real CAD, not meshes

CadQuery builds exact B-rep solids — hinged assemblies, tolerances, STEP export — then a visual check confirms the part came out as described.

Read more

Most AI CAD produces meshes you cannot edit. Amy drives CadQuery to build exact B-rep solids — real geometry with real constraints, exportable to STEP for any CAD package.

How to use it

  • “Model me a hinged enclosure, 80 by 50 by 30 millimetres.”
  • “Make the wall thickness 3 mm and add mounting tabs.” — refines the current part.
  • Edit the generated CadQuery code directly in the CAD panel and re-run it.
  • Export to STEP or STL from the same panel.

Settings

cad.max_attemptsHow many times she retries when the geometry fails to build.
cad.visual_checkRenders the result and checks it against your description before showing it.
cad.slicer_pathPoint this at your slicer to send parts straight to print.

Worth knowing

CadQuery is about 450 MB and is imported lazily — it costs nothing until the first CAD request. Parts land in amy_data/cad/.

Cameras & gestures

Run several named cameras — desk, bench, printer — and switch by voice. Point one at something and ask what it is. Gestures map to actions: open palm pauses media, fist mutes.

Read more

Run as many cameras as you have, give each one a name, and switch between them by voice. Point one at something and ask what it is.

Setting cameras up

  • “Amy, scan for cameras” — probes your devices and adds any new ones.
  • “What cameras do I have?”
  • “Rename the desk camera to bench”
  • “Switch to the bench camera”
  • “Add a camera called printer on device 2”

Or use the dropdown in the camera view to switch, and the pencil to rename.

Using the feed

  • “What is this?” — identify an object held up to the lens.
  • Read text, translate, explain or solve what is in frame, from the camera bar.

Proactive engine

She thinks on a timer, watches folders for new files, and raises things worth raising — rate-limited, and silent during quiet hours.

Read more

Amy thinks on a timer rather than only when spoken to. She watches folders, notices things worth raising, and tells you — within limits you set.

How to use it

  • Suggestions appear as cards you can accept or dismiss.
  • Drop a file into a watched folder and she will offer to do something sensible with it.
  • “Be less chatty” adjusts how often she speaks up.

Settings

proactive.leveloff, low, normal or high.
quiet_hoursStart and end hour during which she stays silent. Defaults to 23–7.
max_per_hourHard ceiling on unprompted interruptions.
think_minutesHow often she reflects on what is going on.
watch_foldersFolders to notice new files in — Downloads and Videos by default.

Memory that persists

Transcripts, a knowledge base and learned skills live on disk in amy_data, watched for changes and recalled in later conversations.

Read more

Conversations, facts and learned skills survive restarts. Everything lives as plain files in amy_data/, so you can read and edit them yourself.

How to use it

  • “Remember that my printer bed is 220 by 220.”
  • “What do you know about my workshop?”
  • “Consolidate your memory” — merges duplicates and prunes stale facts.
  • Drop notes or documents into the knowledge base folder and she picks them up.

What is on disk

SOUL.mdWho she is and how she behaves.
MEMORY.mdThe index of what she knows about you.
HEARTBEAT.mdRunning notes between sessions.
skills/Routines she has learned.
amy_memory.jsonStructured facts, with a backup alongside it.

Worth knowing

amy_data/ is personal and is excluded by .gitignore — do not commit it, and check the debug log for credentials before sharing it.

Model routing

Separate local models for fast replies, reasoning, code, CAD and vision. Auto-select picks the right one per request instead of one model for everything.

Read more

One model for everything is a compromise. Amy keeps separate models for different jobs and picks per request, so quick questions stay quick and hard ones get a model that can handle them.

The roles

fastShort replies, acknowledgements, simple lookups.
chatOrdinary conversation.
reasoningMulti-step problems worth the extra time.
codeWriting and fixing code.
cadGenerating CadQuery geometry.
visionScreen and camera understanding.

How to use it

Leave any role blank and it falls back to the main model. Fill in the ones you care about:

"models": {
  "fast": "llama3.2:1b",
  "reasoning": "qwen2.5:14b",
  "code": "qwen2.5-coder:7b",
  "vision": "llava"
}

Set auto_select to false to pin everything to one model. auto_start launches the Ollama server on boot, and stop_on_exit shuts it down when she closes.

Everything stays home

Inference runs against localhost. Mail, messaging and smart-home integrations are opt-in, and credentials live only in your own config file.

Read more

The assistant is local by construction, not by promise. Inference goes to localhost, and anything that reaches the internet is something you configured yourself.

What never leaves

  • Your voice, and every transcript of it.
  • Screen captures and camera frames.
  • Conversations, memory and knowledge base.
  • Generated code and CAD.

What can reach out, if you set it up

  • Email through your own SMTP account.
  • SMS through your own Twilio credentials.
  • Spotify, Home Assistant, weather and web research.

Where credentials live

Only in amy_config.json, on your machine. The copy in the repository contains placeholders only. If you fork Amy and add real keys, keep that file out of version control.

Architecture

How an offline voice assistant works, wake word to reply

What happens between you saying “Amy” and hearing an answer back.

  1. Wake wordMicrophone, clap detection and voice activity detection decide when she is being spoken to.
  2. Speech to textWhisper or Vosk transcribes locally, on real speech only.
  3. Intent routerGrammar matching and reference resolution work out what you meant.
  4. Local model, skills, agent or visionThe request fans out to whichever subsystem can answer it.
  5. ComposeThe answer is deliberated over and self-checked before it is given.
  6. Voice and cardA short spoken reply through edge-tts, with the full answer on screen.
Always listening, cheaplyVoice activity detection gates the expensive stages, so the transcriber only runs on real speech.
Grammar before guessingConfident phrase matches execute directly; borderline ones get a clarifying question instead of a wrong action.
Approval gatesAnything classified as risky pauses for a yes, with a timeout that defaults to declining.
Spoken replies stay shortThe full answer lands on the on-screen card; the voice gets the summary.
Setup

How to set up a local voice assistant on Windows

Windows, Python 3.10 or newer, and a machine with enough headroom to host a local model.

Clone and install

CadQuery and OpenCV are heavy, but they're imported lazily — nothing loads until you ask for CAD or the camera.

git clone https://github.com/edzzztech/Amy-AI.git
cd Amy-AI
pip install -r requirements.txt

Pull the local models

Install Ollama, then pull a chat model, a vision model and an embedding model. Amy can start the Ollama server herself on launch.

ollama pull llama3.2          # conversation + reasoning
ollama pull llava             # screen and camera vision
ollama pull nomic-embed-text  # memory search

Fill in your config

Open amy_config.json and replace the placeholders. Everything is optional except the Ollama block — skip any integration you don't want and that feature simply stays quiet.

"assistant": {
  "wake_word": "amy",
  "user_title": "Sir",
  "default_city": "London",
  "neural_voice": "en-GB-SoniaNeural"
}

Say hello

She greets you on launch, then waits for the wake word. Say “Amy, what can you do?” to hear the current skill list.

python amy.py

Questions

Common questions about running AI locally

What people usually want to know before installing an offline assistant.

Is Amy AI free?

Yes. The code is free to use, change and share for any noncommercial purpose under the PolyForm Noncommercial licence. There are no API keys, no subscription and no per-request cost, because the models run on your own hardware.

Does it work completely offline?

The assistant itself does. Speech recognition, the language model and the vision model all run locally, so conversation, screen reading and CAD work without a connection. Optional extras that are inherently online — weather, web research, Spotify — need one.

How is this different from Alexa, Siri or ChatGPT?

Those send your voice to a company's servers and can't touch your machine. Amy runs on your PC, sees your screen, opens and drives your applications, and keeps every transcript on your own disk.

What hardware do I need to run a local LLM?

Anything that can host your chosen Ollama model. A small model like llama3.2 runs on 8 GB of RAM; vision and reasoning models want more, and a GPU makes replies noticeably faster. Heavy parts load only when used.

Is any of my data sent anywhere?

Inference goes to localhost. Email, messaging and smart-home integrations are opt-in and only contact services you configure yourself. Credentials stay in your own config file on your machine.

Can it really control my computer?

Yes — it finds and drives real applications through Windows UI automation. Anything classed as risky pauses for your approval first, and shell command execution is off by default.

Does it run on Mac or Linux?

Not yet. The automation layer uses Windows-specific APIs, so Amy currently targets Windows 10 and 11.

Can I use more than one camera?

Yes. Add as many as you like, give each a name, and switch by voice — “scan for cameras”, then “switch to the bench camera”.

Have a look at the source

One Python file, no services, no accounts. Clone it, point it at a local model, and talk to your computer.
